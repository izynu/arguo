## What scholarly mode does

The academic profile sets scholarly_mode, which changes how search queries are built. For each subquestion the planner adds scholarly variants: the keywords followed by "study", "systematic review", or "meta-analysis", and these variants are placed ahead of the plain ones so they survive the per-subquestion query cap. The profile targets six subquestions, runs two search rounds with eight results per query, keeps up to 20 sources, and produces a detailed report that names methods, limitations, and gaps.

## Review resources in the catalog

The catalog's systematic-reviews category holds 7 records, and three matter most for literature work. The Cochrane Library publishes systematic reviews and evidence syntheses. The Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews documents the methods behind them. PROSPERO registers review protocols in advance, which lets you check whether a published review followed its protocol or drifted from it. Academic search resources such as OpenAlex, Europe PMC, and CORE are cataloged under academic-search and bibliographic-metadata.

## Evaluating study quality

Judge each study on three layers. Methods: design, controls, blinding, and whether the analysis matches the design. Sample: size, population, how participants were selected, and whether the sample can support the generalization being claimed. Limitations: those the authors state and the ones they do not. A paper and the press release describing it are one independence group, not two sources. Preprints are not peer reviewed and are cited as preprints. A meta-analysis is stronger than a single study only if its inclusion criteria are sound, so read them.

## How the academic profile differs

Compared with standard, the academic profile searches harder per subquestion, prefers scholarly phrasing, and spends its report budget on methods and limitations. Compared with deep, it does not turn on contradiction focus, though contradictions found along the way are still recorded. Use deep when the question is contested and scattered across non-scholarly sources; use academic when the answer should rest on the literature.

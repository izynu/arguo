## Where to start

Four catalog categories carry most of the load. corporate-filings (15 records) includes SEC EDGAR, Companies House, OpenCorporates, and GLEIF. financial-markets (15) covers market and price data. economics-finance (29) holds official statistical series. trade-supply-chain (11) covers cross border goods data. For a company question, the registry and the filings come before the company's own website.

## Filings versus narrative

An annual report, a periodic filing, a prospectus, or a registry extract is a primary record: signed, dated, and usually audited or officially received. A founder interview, a product page, or a launch post is a claim by an interested party. The catalog records an official_source flag when the publisher is the originating authority for the data; 1,401 of 1,570 current records carry it. The flag describes the publisher, not the truth of any single number, so filings still get read with care.

## Marketing claims and their bases

Marketing language such as "fastest growing" or "market leader" rarely states a definable base. When a company cites a market share, find the study it rests on: the definition of the market, the period, and the method. If the basis cannot be found, keep the sentence but label it as a company assertion in the claim-evidence table rather than as a measured fact.

## Corroboration across independent domains

A press release and every outlet reprinting it are one independence group; corroborating a launch across reprints is not corroboration. A filing read together with an independent analyst's report is two groups. For market size numbers, check whether the series exists in an official statistics or trade data source before quoting a private estimate, and record which numbers are official series and which are modeled estimates.

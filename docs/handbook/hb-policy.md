## Legislation portals first

law-regulation is the catalog's law anchor with 25 records: EUR-Lex for EU law, legislation.gov.uk for UK legislation as it stands, the eCFR for the US Code of Federal Regulations, the Federal Register, Congress.gov, govinfo, CourtListener, CanLII, and the United Nations Treaty Collection. international-relations-policy adds 15 more resources for treaties, indicators, and policy data. Start at the portal that publishes the text itself.

## The official_source flag

The catalog schema carries an official_source field, true when the publisher is the originating authority for the content. For legal questions this flag is the first filter: commentary sites can explain a statute, but only the official portal is the source of the text in force. The flag describes the publisher's role, not the correctness of any interpretation you place on the text.

## Reading regulatory text

Identify the version in force on the date that matters, the entry into force provisions, and the amendments that apply. A citation to "the regulation" without a version and a date is not checkable, so record both in the ledger row. For judicial positions, prefer the judgment text over any summary of it, and quote the operative holding rather than a headline.

## Assessment bodies and commentary

When a policy rests on a scientific or technical claim, the scientific-assessments category (8 records) points at independent assessment bodies; cite the assessment report itself, not the coverage of it. Commentary still has a place: explanatory memoranda, agency guidance, and reputable analysis help you understand effect and intent. Record them as context rows in the ledger, keep load-bearing claims anchored in the legal text, and say plainly when the text is ambiguous rather than resolving the ambiguity silently.

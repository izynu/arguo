## The checklist per number

Every number that carries weight gets six items captured: the value, the unit, the denominator or base, the reference period, the geography, and the revision status. A percentage without its base, a total without its period, or a growth rate without its comparison window cannot be verified, and quoting it as if it could is overreach. Percentages of different bases are not comparable just because the numbers look alike; watch also for rates confused with counts and per person figures confused with per household figures.

## The numeric contradiction detector

When two sources give different values for the same quantity, the contradiction step records both in the contradiction table instead of choosing quietly. Each conflict gets a classification: measurement difference, definitional difference, temporal difference, scope difference, error, or genuine uncertainty. The deep profile turns contradiction focus on, and contradiction records are first class ledger rows that never get deleted for tidiness. Resolution, when reached, is recorded with its basis; when no resolution is reached, the report says so and carries both values.

## Revisions and vintages

Official series are revised, and a claim can be true of one vintage and false of the next. Record the as-of date and, where the vintage matters, cite the archival version of the series rather than the latest one.

## Precision honesty

Keep the precision the source gives, round at most once, and never let a summary gain digits in transit. Confidence stays qualitative: high, moderate, low, or insufficient, each with a reason tied to corroboration and source quality. A number like "87 percent confident" is false precision and not part of this method; the honest statement is the level and why.

## Choosing the right kind of data

Environmental questions often fail because two different data products are treated as interchangeable. Station records are observations at fixed points. Reanalysis products, such as those behind Copernicus C3S, are model reconstructions constrained by observations, and they answer area-wide questions that sparse stations cannot. Satellite products measure what the sensor measures, with their own calibration histories. Model projections answer what-if questions, not what happened. Say which kind of product a number comes from.

Species and land claims have their own traps: GBIF occurrences show where something was recorded, not everywhere it lives, and forest change figures depend on the definition of forest in use.

The categories behind this spotlight also hold meteorological services, biodiversity programs, and energy and agriculture collections, which are covered by their own categories in the taxonomy.

government-statistics is the catalog's second largest category with 40 records, and economics-finance holds 29. These twelve are frequent starting points for official numbers.

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| World Bank Open Data | Free cross-country development indicators with documentation and a public API. |
| IMF Data | International monetary, financial, exchange rate, and fiscal statistics. |
| OECD Data | Comparable indicators across OECD members and partner economies. |
| FRED | US economic time series from the St. Louis Fed, with ALFRED holding earlier vintages. |
| Eurostat | European Union statistics across economy, population, and environment. |
| UNData | Search across the statistical databases of UN agencies. |
| US Census Bureau | US population, business, and trade statistics. |
| ONS | UK official statistics: inflation, GDP, labour market, and population. |
| Destatis | Germany's federal statistical office. |
| Statistics Canada | Canada's national statistical agency. |
| BEA | US national, industry, and regional economic accounts, including GDP. |
| BIS Statistics | International banking and monetary statistics from the Bank for International Settlements. |

Two habits apply to all of them. First, check the reference period and the revision status before quoting: series are revised, and ALFRED exists precisely because the vintage of a series can matter as much as its value. Second, prefer the statistical office's own definition pages when a term such as "unemployed" or "household income" carries the claim; definitions differ across agencies and across time.

The full category lists include national statistical offices from many more countries; browse the government-statistics category in the explorer or with resources list.

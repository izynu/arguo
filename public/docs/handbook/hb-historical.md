## Where to look

The catalog's historical coverage spans four categories. history-archives (15 records) includes the US National Archives, The National Archives UK, the Library of Congress, Europeana, and topic collections such as Old Bailey Online. national-libraries (15) adds digitized national collections. news-archives (10) holds digitized newspapers: Chronicling America, Trove Newspapers, Gallica, Europeana Newspapers, and others. web-archives (8), including the Wayback Machine, covers the recent past of the web itself.

## Date boundaries

Four dates matter and are not interchangeable: the event date, the publication date, the observation period, and the update or revision date. A newspaper article published three days after a fire still describes the fire, but it may also contain errors the fire corrected. A finding aid written decades later describes the archive's arrangement, not the event. Record which date a ledger row carries, and never let a page's freshness stand in for the reference period of the data on it.

## Digitized newspapers

Optical character recognition corrupts search results, so search several spellings and name variants, and never treat an absent hit as evidence that an event went unreported. Coverage is uneven by region, language, and period: state what the archive you searched actually covers so the absence is interpretable.

## Conflicting interpretations

Historians disagree, and the disagreement is data. Record contradicting rows in the ledger and classify the difference: different sources, different periods, different definitions, or genuine uncertainty. Prefer the primary document where one survives, and note the context in which each account was produced, because who wrote a record and why is part of what it evidences. Interpretation sentences cite the claims they rest on and stay separate from fact sentences.

## The fifteen required elements

A report built from the report template must contain all of the following. The first seven are header and scope fields; the rest are sections. None may be deleted, and a missing limitations section is a defect, not a convenience.

1. Date
2. Prepared by
3. As-of date for current-state claims
4. Question
5. Interpretation used
6. Scope: jurisdiction, population, units, time boundary
7. Depth profile
8. Bottom line
9. Claim-evidence table
10. Findings by subquestion
11. Contradictions
12. Limitations
13. Integrity note
14. Sources
15. Appendix: search trail summary

The bottom line is written last and may not exceed what the findings and the claim-evidence table support. Confidence is stated qualitatively with reasons, per claim and overall.

## Citation identifiers

Sources carry S-numbers, ledger evidence rows carry E-numbers, and claims carry C-numbers, assigned in sequence. Every factual sentence cites at least one source ID, written as [S1] and similar; claims synthesized from several sources cite all of them. Quotes appear verbatim in quotation marks with a locator. Source list rows give title, publisher, publication date or "undated", the canonical URL, and the retrieval date.

## Background sources

Context that bears on a claim without supporting or contradicting it is recorded in the claim-evidence table with the relation "context". Every listed source must be used by at least one claim; an unused source is removed or put to work. Numerical claims cite the origin of the number, not the most convenient repetition of it.

## What the audit summary means

The pre-delivery audit checks for missing-source, unused-source, invalid-url, fabricated-citation, unsupported-claim, duplicate-source, and missing-metadata issues, and failing citations are fixed or removed. The appendix search trail summary records queries run, sources retrieved, ledger rows written, and where the full log lives, so a reader can retrace the run end to end.

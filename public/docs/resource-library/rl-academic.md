The catalog's academic-search category holds 25 records and bibliographic-metadata another 20. These ten are the common starting points for literature questions.

| Resource | Best for |
|---|---|
| OpenAlex | Open metadata on papers, authors, institutions, and citation links, with a free API. |
| Crossref | DOI metadata for scholarly works, including funders, licenses, and references. |
| PubMed | Biomedical citations from the National Library of Medicine, the standard MEDLINE entry point. |
| Europe PMC | Biomedical literature with integrated full text, covering PubMed content and more. |
| arXiv | Preprints in physics, mathematics, computer science, and adjacent fields. |
| DOAJ | Directory of quality-controlled open-access journals. |
| OpenAIRE | Aggregated outputs from European and international repositories. |
| CORE | Large aggregation of open-access papers drawn from repositories and journals. |
| BASE | Bielefeld search index over institutional repository documents. |
| Semantic Scholar | Literature search with citation contexts and paper level influence data. |

A few habits make these effective. Preprints are not peer reviewed, so a preprint and its published version are the same work in two states, and you cite the one you actually read. Aggregators such as BASE, CORE, and OpenAIRE can lead to the repository copy of a paywalled paper. Citation indexes such as OpenAlex, Crossref, and Semantic Scholar are metadata services: their records are usually accurate, but the underlying claim still gets checked in the paper itself.

For systematic reviews and trial evidence, the health spotlight in this library covers the review registries and trial registers, including the Cochrane Library and PROSPERO.

# Literature reviews

A literature review answers "what does the accumulated research say" rather
than "what does one source say". The method differs from ordinary research in
one decisive way: the search protocol is written down before searching, so
the set of studies examined is defensible.

## Choose the review type

- Narrative review: an organized survey of a topic, selection judgmental,
  transparent about being so. Suitable for orientation.
- Systematic review: pre-defined criteria, reproducible search, documented
  screening. Required when the conclusion will carry weight.
- Scoping review: maps what exists and where the gaps are, without deep
  quality appraisal of each item.

State the type at the start. Never let a narrative review dress up as
systematic.

## Write the protocol first

- Inclusion criteria: study designs, populations, interventions or exposures,
  outcomes, date range, languages, publication types.
- Exclusion criteria, with reasons: retracted items, non-empirical
  commentary, wrong population, and so on.
- Databases and catalogs to search, including the repo catalog at
  data/resources/ where categories like systematic-reviews or
  biomedical-literature apply.
- Search strings per database, recorded verbatim, including filters and
  limits.

## Search execution

- Record, per source database: the string, the date run, and the count of
  hits. A reviewer should be able to rerun the search.
- Snowball after the database passes: backward chasing through reference
  lists, forward chasing through citing works. Add new records only through
  the same inclusion criteria.
- Cover grey literature deliberately: preprints, theses, agency and NGO
  reports, trial registries. Label items grey; their peer-review status
  differs.

## Screening

- Screen in stages: titles, then abstracts, then full text. At each stage,
  apply the written criteria and count what was excluded and why.
- The flow of records, from hits to included studies, goes into the report.
  Undocumented exclusions are how cherry-picking enters a review unnoticed.

## Appraisal and synthesis

- Appraise each included study on method fit: design, sampling, measures,
  analysis, and limitations. The rubric criteria in
  references/source-quality.md apply here per study.
- Build a study characteristics table: design, population, period, outcome
  measures, effect sizes as reported.
- Heterogeneity is information: differing populations, measures, and designs
  limit what can be combined. Do not pool or average across studies whose
  comparability has not been argued.
- Distinguish what the body of evidence shows, what single studies show, and
  what remains contested. State the direction and strength of agreement.

## Retractions and corrections

- Check retraction and correction status for every load-bearing study before
  delivery; a retracted paper can still be circulating widely.
- Record the check in limitations, especially when the review rests on
  fields with known retraction problems.

## Stopping and reporting

- Stop when the protocol's stopping rule is met: two consecutive rounds with
  no new included studies, or budget exhaustion.
- Report: protocol, search counts, screening flow, included studies,
  synthesis, limitations, and what would change the conclusion.

## Checklist

- [ ] Review type stated.
- [ ] Protocol written before searching, with criteria and strings.
- [ ] Search dates and hit counts recorded per database.
- [ ] Screening stages counted and reported.
- [ ] Grey literature covered or its absence noted.
- [ ] Study table and heterogeneity discussion present.
- [ ] Retraction status checked.

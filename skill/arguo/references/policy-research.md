# Policy and legal research

Legal and policy questions are version-sensitive, jurisdiction-sensitive, and
sensitive to exactly which text counts. The discipline is to cite the binding
text itself, in force, in the right jurisdiction, at the right version.

## Hierarchy of instruments

Within a jurisdiction, authority usually runs from constitutional text, to
statutes passed by the legislature, to regulations and executive instruments,
to guidance and circulars that interpret but do not bind. Local rules sit
under national ones. When instruments conflict, the hierarchy, not the
eloquence of the text, decides.

## Official texts

- Use the official source: national gazettes, legislative portals, and
  official law publication systems. The repo catalog's law-regulation and
  international-relations-policy categories list the main portals.
- Unofficial aggregators are convenient and lag: amendments, corrections,
  and repeals can be missing. Use them to find the instrument, then verify
  the text officially.
- Court decisions: use the court's own database or the official reporter
  where possible.

## Version and in-force status

- Laws change. Record the version consolidated at a date, or the original
  text plus the amendments, and say which you used.
- Check in-force status: passed but not yet effective, effective, amended,
  repealed, struck down, or lapsed by sunset clause. A still-circulating page
  can describe a repealed instrument without saying so.
- Effective dates can differ from passage dates by months; both go in the
  ledger when relevant.

## Court decisions

- Subsequent history matters: a decision may be reversed, upheld, or
  distinguished later. Check citator information or later cases before
  citing it as standing law.
- Cite the decision by case name, court, date, and identifier or number as
  the jurisdiction's convention requires.
- Distinguish holdings (what the court decided) from dicta and from later
  commentary on the case.

## Binding text versus commentary

- Explanatory memoranda, press releases, law-firm briefings, and NGO
  summaries explain; they do not bind. They are context sources, and the
  report should say so.
- When a summary and the statutory text differ, the text wins, and the
  divergence is worth recording.
- Drafts and bills are not law. Label them as drafts with their stage.

## Jurisdiction discipline

- Fix the jurisdiction early: national, state or provincial, municipal.
- Never blend rules from different jurisdictions into one composite claim.
  Comparative statements must name each jurisdiction and its instrument.
- Supranational instruments sit alongside national ones; record the
  relationship the instrument itself claims.

## Recording specifics

Per legal claim, the ledger should support retrieval: instrument name,
instrument type, jurisdiction, identifier (act number, regulation number,
case number), version or amendment date, section or article numbers, and
in-force status at the as-of date of the report.

This skill organizes and cites legal sources; it does not provide legal
advice. Where a question requires advice on a concrete situation, say so and
point the requester to a qualified professional.

## Checklist

- [ ] Official text used and cited by identifier and section.
- [ ] Version and amendment status recorded.
- [ ] In-force status confirmed at the report's as-of date.
- [ ] Subsequent history checked for case citations.
- [ ] Binding text distinguished from commentary everywhere.
- [ ] Jurisdiction named on every legal claim.

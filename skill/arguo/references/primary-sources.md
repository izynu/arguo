# Primary sources

A primary source is the originating record of the thing being claimed: the
study itself, the filing itself, the statute itself. Secondary sources report
on primaries; tertiary sources summarize secondaries. Load-bearing claims
should rest on primaries, or on independent secondaries that agree.

## Why secondary sources drift

Each retelling compresses, rounds, and re-contextualizes. A headline drops the
denominator; a blog drops the reference year; an aggregator drops the
population restriction. By the third remove, the claim often survives only in
a form the primary would not support. Tracing back is how you find out.

## Primary source types by domain

- Science and medicine: the journal article, the preprint, the clinical trial
  registry entry, the dataset, the conference paper. Not the press release,
  not the news article about the study.
- Statistics: the statistical agency's series, table, or bulletin. Not a news
  chart of the agency's numbers.
- Law and policy: the statute text in the gazette or legislative portal, the
  regulation, the court opinion. Not a summary from a law firm blog.
- Corporate facts: the annual report, the filing, the audited accounts. Not
  the press release headline, not a news summary of earnings.
- History: contemporaneous documents, letters, minutes, photographs,
  newspapers from the period, archived records.
- Events and statements: the full transcript or video, the archived page, the
  official statement. Not the quote-of-a-quote.

## Locating primaries

- Mine the references of secondary sources: every serious secondary names its
  primaries. Follow them.
- Search registry identifiers: study registration numbers, statute numbers,
  filing identifiers, dataset DOIs. Identifiers lead to the primary directly.
- Go to the originating institution's own site, and from there to its
  publications, filings, or records section.
- Use web archives to reach pages that have moved or been withdrawn, and to
  see what a page said at the time.

## Syndication and mirrors

- A press release republished by fifty outlets is one document.
- An article carried by a wire service and reprinted under different headlines
  is one source with many faces.
- Mirrors of the same PDF are one source. Record the canonical version,
  preferably from the origin's own domain.

## When secondary is acceptable

- No primary is publicly accessible; record that limitation explicitly.
- The secondary is itself the object of study, for example when researching
  how an event was reported.
- The secondary is independent, transparent about its methods, and no primary
  claims under dispute rest solely on it.

In all three cases, name the secondary as secondary in the ledger and note
what the primary would be and where it might live.

## Checklist for every load-bearing claim

- [ ] Primary identified or the absence recorded as a limitation.
- [ ] Canonical version used, not a mirror or repost.
- [ ] Registry or identifier recorded when one exists.
- [ ] Press releases treated as the subject's own claims, not independent
      evidence.
- [ ] Quote taken from the primary, not from a secondary quoting it.

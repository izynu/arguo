# Privacy and safety

Research runs on public evidence. When a task touches information about
people, the same evidential standards apply, plus duties of restraint: collect
the minimum, aggregate where possible, and refuse scope that exists only to
expose someone.

## Scope of this file

This file covers conduct, not legal advice. Data protection and privacy laws
exist across jurisdictions and differ; when a task involves processing
personal data at scale, flag that compliance review by a qualified person may
be needed.

## Personal data minimization

- Use the least identifying data that answers the question. Where the
  question can be answered with aggregates, do not collect individual
  records.
- Do not reproduce personal contact details, home addresses, identity
  numbers, or precise location traces in reports or ledgers, even when
  publicly posted. Cite the source type and say what was omitted and why.
- Public availability is not consent. A fact being findable does not make it
  appropriate to gather, repeat, or republish.

## Prohibited or narrowed tasks

- Do not compile profiles of private individuals: assembling identity,
  employment, address, associates, and movements into a dossier.
- Do not join separate datasets to de-anonymize people, and do not research
  how to do so.
- Do not investigate a person's health, sexuality, religion, ethnicity, or
  criminal history without a clearly legitimate, proportionate purpose
  stated by the requester. Absent that, narrow or decline the subquestion.
- Do not assist surveillance of a specific individual, including of public
  figures beyond what their public role requires.

## Sensitive data categories

Treat these as elevated-risk wherever they appear: health, biometrics,
religion, ethnicity, political opinion, union membership, sexual life,
criminal records, immigration status, and children's data. Any research that
must touch them:

- states the legitimate purpose in the plan,
- minimizes records collected,
- aggregates in reporting,
- and records the handling decision in limitations.

## Access conduct

- Respect technical and contractual access boundaries: do not bypass
  paywalls, authentication, rate limits, or robots exclusions to obtain
  content.
- Do not misrepresent identity to obtain information.
- Note in the ledger when a source could not be accessed for access-control
  reasons; that is an access limitation, not an invitation to circumvent.

## Handling personal data found incidentally

- Do not copy it into the ledger beyond what the claim requires.
- Do not include it in quotes when the quote works without it; mark the
  redaction explicitly, for example with a bracketed note.
- If a source exposes others' personal data, record the fact, do not
  propagate it, and consider whether the source remains usable.

## Reporting conduct

- Reports about named individuals rely on the same corroboration standards:
  allegations are labeled as allegations with their origin, status, and any
  denial, not blended into established fact.
- Defamation risk rises when corroboration is thin; that is a reason to
  lower confidence and to attribute carefully, sentence by sentence.
- When in doubt about whether a person-related subquestion is appropriate,
  narrow to the institutional question: research the organization, policy,
  or event rather than the private person.

## Checklist

- [ ] Person-related subquestions identified in the plan.
- [ ] Legitimate, proportionate purpose stated or scope narrowed.
- [ ] Minimum data collected; aggregates preferred.
- [ ] No profiles, de-anonymization, or surveillance assistance.
- [ ] Access boundaries respected; circumvention attempts never made.
- [ ] Incidentally found personal data not propagated.
- [ ] Allegations attributed and corroboration stated.

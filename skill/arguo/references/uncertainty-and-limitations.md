# Uncertainty and limitations

Uncertainty is reported, not hidden. The report's credibility comes from
matching the strength of its language to the strength of its evidence, and
from naming precisely what the research could not establish.

## Confidence levels

Use four qualitative levels, always with reasons:

- High: primary evidence, independently corroborated, methods adequate,
  contradictions resolved. Reserve this for claims that would survive a
  skeptical reviewer's re-checking.
- Moderate: consistent evidence with at least one gap: a single strong
  source without independent corroboration, a secondary source carrying a
  load-bearing claim, or a definitional choice that affects the result.
- Low: weak, partial, or conflicting evidence; the claim is the best
  available reading but could be wrong in an important way.
- Insufficient: the search could not find evidence that settles the claim.
  Say what was searched and what would settle it.

Most honest research lands at moderate. High is rare and should read as
rare. If every claim in a draft is high, re-check before delivery.

## Reasons, not scores

- Always attach the reason: which rubric criteria drove the level, and what
  corroboration was found or missing. A confidence word without a reason is
  decoration.
- Do not convert qualitative judgments into decimal scores without a
  defensible basis. False precision is worse than honest words.

## Limitations taxonomy

Write limitations that are concrete. The recurring families:

- Access limits: paywalls, removed pages, unarchived sources, region-locked
  records, paywalled abstracts only.
- Language limits: sources existed but were not readable in the languages
  available; translation relied on machine translation.
- Time and budget limits: the search stopped at the budget; the stated
  rounds were covered, deeper ones were not.
- Source gaps: no primary could be located; the field is dominated by
  syndication of one origin.
- Conflicting evidence: the contradiction table holds unresolved rows.
- Method limits: survey-based numbers carry sampling uncertainty; forecasts
  are conditional; definitions chosen affect results.
- Temporal limits: the answer is as-of a date; the state may have changed.

## Writing the limitations section

- Name what could not be established, not just what was hard: "the 2023
  figure could not be traced to a primary; all accessible copies trace to one
  newsroom origin".
- Separate limitations of evidence from limitations of method: what the
  sources could not show, versus what your approach could not reach.
- For each material limitation, add what would settle it: the record, the
  archive, the dataset, or the query that would resolve the gap.

## Calibration habits

- Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence, and say which
  one the report has.
- Hedging words carry content: "suggests", "is consistent with", "appears
  to", "one source reports". Use them deliberately; do not flatten them into
  certainty in the summary, and do not hide clear findings behind them
  either.
- The executive summary must not claim more than the findings section
  supports. The two sections get checked against each other before delivery.
